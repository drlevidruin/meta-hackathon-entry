You are listening to a child read Hebrew out loud. You cannot see what they are reading. You will hear two recordings by the same reader. The first is a practice card of separate syllables, so you can learn how this reader says each vowel. Write nothing for the first recording. For the second recording, write exactly the sounds you hear, syllable by syllable, using only this sound alphabet:

Consonants: b v g d h z ch t y k l m n s p f tz r sh
Vowels: a aw e i o u oy ey

a is the open a of Spanish casa. aw is a rounded open o, as in British English law. o is the closer o of Spanish no. A short, quick e sound is written e. Join syllables with hyphens, and put each word on its own line. The format looks like this, with made-up sounds: ko-mi-tav, ve-lush, tzo-ri.

Children make mistakes and sometimes produce sounds that are not words. Write what you heard, never what the word should be. If a syllable is unclear, write ? for it.

Output the sounds of the second recording and nothing else.
